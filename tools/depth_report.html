Summary

Headline numbers per run. Bits = log2 of distinct stored codes over the sweep; p99 = 99th percentile of the relative vertex error |dw|/w against the fitted mapping Z = A + B/w.

Stored Z vs distance SWEEP, 2000 log-spaced steps, one flat quad each

"w" throughout is the view-space depth of the test quad in world units (camera-space −z; for a perspective projection this equals clip-space W, which is how the ROM reports it). All runs render the very same depths, so runs can be compared sample by sample. Difference to reference: stored Z of each run minus the reference, either in integer Z units (code space: 1 unit = 1/32767 of the depth range, where the pipeline loses bits) or converted to a distance error, either in % of w or in plain world units (what a code is worth on screen: dw = dZ·w²/B, so one code is 0.003 units near the camera but ~300 units at the far plane of 10/12800), or in z-buffer codes: the Z difference divided by the format's resolution at that depth (8 Z units below Z = 16384, then 4, 2, 1, ½, ¼, ⅛ at the top), i.e. how many storable values the vertex is off by. The reference is another run, the exact analytic mapping Z = 32767·(zndc+1)/2, that mapping through the RDP's z-buffer format (s15.16 → u15.3 → 11-bit-mantissa float, as angrylion does it; the hardware floor nobody can change), or additionally with the W-norm quantisation both ucodes share (w·2/(far+near) held in s15.16 before the reciprocal; the near-field curve with its sign flip at zclip = 0, w = 2fn/(f+n)). Step to previous sample: how much the stored Z moved between neighbouring sweep steps, i.e. the quantisation grain. Fit residual: distance from a global Z = A + B/w fit; use it only for the envelope. Dots mark inversions (stored Z decreases while w increases).

Resolution vs distance distinct stored codes per ½ octave of w

How many different depth values the sweep produced inside each half-octave of distance. Dashed grey: sweep samples in the bin (the most that could be distinguished). Dotted grey: what the ideal mapping yields after the RDP's z-buffer format, i.e. the hardware ceiling nobody can change. The gap between a run and the dotted line is the loss inside the ucode.

Stored Z staircase raw stored depth, integer units (0..32767)

Zoom into a window of the sweep to see the actual quantisation steps. Integer-only vertex Z produces flat plateaus; fractional Z produces finer steps. Non-monotonic segments are inversions. Dotted grey: the ideal mapping after the RDP z-buffer format (the hardware grid).

Inversions how far beyond a surface can something still draw in front of it

Stored Z must grow with distance. For every sweep sample at distance d this finds the farthest sample whose stored Z is smaller (inversion: that farther surface would be drawn in front) and the farthest one whose Z is not larger (tie: order between the two cannot be resolved, whoever draws last wins). The value plotted is how far beyond d that reaches, in world units (or % of d): "a surface up to this much farther away can still end up in front of one at d". Filled marks = inversions, hollow marks = ties; samples with nothing beyond them are not drawn. The sweep is sampled every 0.36 % of d, so extents below that are not resolvable and ties of one step are the normal quantisation plateau, not a defect. Counts and worst cases per run are in the legend.

Ambiguity span at this depth, how far back and forth is another surface not sorted correctly

For a surface at distance d: back = how far towards the camera another surface can be while its stored Z is still not smaller (it would not win the depth test although it is nearer); forward = how far away another surface can be while its stored Z is still not larger (it could still be drawn in front). Their sum is the span of distances that alias with d, in world units or % of d: inside it, sorting is decided by draw order or is plain wrong. Thin lines are a running percentile over the window; faint dots are the raw per-sample values. Dotted grey: the span the z-buffer format alone imposes (one code at the ideal Z, converted to distance). The sweep is sampled every 0.36 % of d, so nothing below that is resolvable.
